Review Extortion Scams: What to Do if Someone Threatens Your Rating

It usually starts with a few one-star reviews from accounts you have never seen. Then a message arrives: pay, and they’ll stop. It is called review extortion, and it is common enough that Google built dedicated protections and a reporting form for it.

Taploe TeamOct 31, 2026 · 5 min read

How the scam works

Scammers post a burst of fake negative reviews, then contact the owner by WhatsApp, email or Telegram offering to remove them for a fee, often paid in gift cards or crypto. Sometimes they pose as a “reputation agency” that can fix the problem.

Step 1: don’t pay and don’t negotiate

Paying marks you as a target, and there is no guarantee the reviews disappear. Do not reply to the extortion message beyond saving it.

Step 2: document everything

  • Screenshot each fake review with the date and reviewer name.
  • Screenshot the payment demand, including the phone number or account.
  • Note when the attack started.

Step 3: report it to Google

Google has a dedicated form for review-based extortion in its Business Profile help center. Attach your screenshots. Since April 2026, Google also detects many of these spikes automatically, removes the content and can temporarily pause new reviews on your profile while it investigates.

Step 4: reply calmly, if at all

If a fake review stays up for a while, a short, neutral reply is fine: “We have no record of this visit and have reported this review to Google.” Do not mention the extortion or argue.

Step 5: build a buffer of real reviews

The best defense is a steady stream of genuine reviews. A profile that gets new five-star reviews every week absorbs an attack far better than one that hasn’t heard from a customer in months. Put a review card where customers already pay or wait.

Frequently asked questions

Should I pay to have fake reviews removed?

No. Paying rarely works and invites more demands. Report the extortion to Google instead.

Will Google remove the reviews?

Google removes reviews that violate its fake engagement policy. Reports with evidence of extortion help it act faster.

Should I report it to the police?

Extortion is a crime. Filing a report with local authorities or the FBI’s IC3 creates a record and is worth doing, especially if money was demanded.
